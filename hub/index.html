Ground Truth: City on Trial

Every city's audited books, presented as exhibits

Volume One put companies and institutions on the stand with their own filings. Volume Two puts cities there. Every year, every city publishes an audited financial report — hundreds of pages almost nobody reads. City on Trial reads it and presents the record as a case file: the money in and out, the debt, the tax dollar, the payroll, the reserve, the growth. It presents the record and never renders a verdict — the resident is the jury. Case 001: Greenville, SC. South Carolina's docket is complete — 37 cities; North Carolina's 89 cities are verified and entering the docket now; Georgia follows.

What the platforms publish about their own filtering

Carriers and big tech publish transparency reports about the content they filter and the demands they answer. The reports can't be compared — and the gaps are the exhibit. Per-company cards, no league table, every figure from the company's own report.

Ground Truth: People on Trial

Your household's share of your city's debt

City on Trial puts the city in the dock. People on Trial puts your household there. Pick your city and see the share of its bonded debt that rides on a household like yours — computed from the same audited reports, city by city: $169 a household in Carrboro, $5,947 in Kannapolis, $28,401 in Atlanta. On the personal-scale thread: a city's debt, divided down to one household.
